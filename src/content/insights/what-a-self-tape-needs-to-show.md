A self-tape is not a showreel. It answers one question for the person watching: can this performer do the thing we are casting, and will they be easy to direct on the day? Everything in the tape should serve that.

Casting teams watch these in batches, often at speed. Clear beats clever.

## Get the brief right first

Read the request twice and follow it exactly. If it asks for a slate, a full-body shot, or a specific skill demonstrated, that is what gets checked first, and a missing element is the quickest way to be passed over.

- Match the file name they asked for. If they gave no format, use your name and the role.
- Send the file type requested, at the length requested. Do not go over.
- If you genuinely cannot meet part of the brief, tell us rather than quietly skipping it.

## The technical basics

None of this needs equipment you do not already own.

- **Light** falls on your face, not behind you. A window in front of you beats any lamp.
- **Sound** matters more than picture. Close a door, turn off the extractor fan, and record somewhere soft.
- **Framing** is steady and level, phone on something solid, in landscape unless told otherwise.
- **Background** is plain and uncluttered. A blank wall is fine.

## Movement and skills

For dance, fight and physical work, the frame has to hold the whole body with room at the edges. Show the skill cleanly and in full, once, at the speed you would actually perform it, not a cut-together montage.

If the role needs a specific ability, demonstrate it in the tape rather than describing it. One clean pass tells them more than a paragraph.

## Before you send

Watch it back once as if you were the person casting. Is the sound clean? Can they see your face and your feet where it matters? Does anything need explaining? If the answer to the last one is yes, re-record it rather than sending a note with it.
