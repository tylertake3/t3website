A first day on a professional set is mostly waiting, punctuated by short bursts where everything matters. Knowing the shape of the day takes the nerves out of it, and lets you spend your attention on the work rather than on wondering what happens next.

What follows is the ordinary rhythm of a shooting day on a drama or a commercial in the UK. Every production runs slightly differently, and your call sheet is always the final word.

## Before you leave the house

Read the call sheet twice. Check your call time, the unit base address rather than the location address, and whether you have been asked to arrive already dressed or to bring options.

- Bring the kit you were asked for, plus the neutral basics: nude underwear, plain socks, and dance shoes if they are relevant to the role.
- Take a phone charger and something to read. There will be more downtime than you expect.
- Eat before you travel. Breakfast at base is not guaranteed to still be there when you arrive.

## Arriving and signing in

Find the second or third assistant director first. They are the people who own your day. You will sign in, be walked to costume, and given a rough idea of when you are needed on set. From that point on, stay reachable, and tell someone before you go anywhere.

## Costume, hair and make-up

Continuity matters more than comfort here. Once your look is set it is photographed and expected to stay that way, so ask before you take a jacket off, tie your hair back, or roll a sleeve.

## On the floor

Blocking comes first, then lighting, then takes. You may run the same few seconds a dozen times. Match what you did last time as closely as you can. That repeatability is the skill being bought.

- Watch for the first AD's cues, and go quiet the moment you hear "turning over".
- If something hurts or feels unsafe, say so straight away, to the AD or the coordinator.
- Never move a prop or reset a mark yourself. Tell the department whose job it is.

## Wrap

Return everything you were given, sign out, and check whether you are needed again. If anything about the day was unclear (hours, travel, or a change to what you were asked to do), tell us rather than settling it on the floor. That is what we are here for.
