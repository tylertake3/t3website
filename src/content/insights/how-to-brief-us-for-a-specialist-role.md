Most briefs that come back for a second round are missing one of five things. None of them take long to write, and together they usually remove a whole day from the process.

## Say what the performer has to do

Not the character, the action. "Trained ballet dancer, en pointe, on a rostrum" gets a usable shortlist. "Dancer" does not. Where a skill has to be genuine on camera, say so, and say how much of it will be seen.

## Give the dates, including the fittings

Shoot dates alone are not enough to check availability properly. Include rehearsal days, fittings and any travel, and flag which of them are moveable. Performers regularly turn out to be free for the shoot and unavailable for the fitting.

## Be specific about look only where it matters

If the role is a picture double, sizes and stature are the brief. If it isn't, an open description gets you a wider and usually better shortlist. Tell us which of the details are firm and which are a starting point.

## Name the usage up front

Medium, territory and term. This changes the fee, and quoting it late is the most common reason a booking stalls after a performer has been chosen.

## Tell us the shape of the day

Hours, location, whether there is nudity or intimate content, whether stunts or a coordinator are involved, and anything physically demanding. We will raise the right questions with the performer before they accept rather than on the day.

---

Send all of it in one message and we will usually come back the same working day. If the brief is still forming, tell us what you know. An early conversation is more useful than a complete brief that arrives late.
