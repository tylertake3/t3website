Availability language is one of the few parts of this industry that is genuinely confusing, and getting it wrong costs work. Here is what the terms mean in practice.

## Pencil

A pencil is a production noting that they may want you on a date. It is not a booking, and it does not pay. You are free to accept other work, but you should tell us before you do, so we can go back to them rather than let a date quietly disappear.

Pencils are sometimes described as heavy or light. Treat it as a signal of confidence, not as a contract.

## First refusal

A first refusal is a stronger version of the same thing: if another job wants the same date, the production holding the refusal gets asked first, and has to either confirm or let you go. That conversation is ours to have, not yours. Pass it to us and we will handle it the same day.

## Confirmation

A confirmation is the booking. It carries the date, the fee, the hours and the usage. From that point, turning it down has consequences for you and for us, so only accept when you are certain of the date.

- Check the travel and the call time before you accept, not after.
- Tell us about anything that could clash, including recalls for other jobs.
- Keep the confirmation email. It is the record if anything is disputed later.

## Release

A release is the production formally letting a date go. Until you hear it from us, assume the date is still held. A rumour on set or a quiet week is not a release.

## Buyouts and usage

Separate from availability, your fee usually covers a defined use of the footage: the medium, the territory and the length of time it can run. If a production later wants more than that, it is renegotiated. Never agree usage on the floor; send it to us.

If you are ever unsure which of these you have been given, ask. It takes one message and it protects the date.
