Stand-ins & picture doubles

Accurate matches to your leads, experienced on set — so lighting holds, cameras keep turning and your actors' hours go where they matter.

THE DIFFERENCE

Six jobs, often confused. Here's what each one does.

01

Stand-in

Takes the actor's place while shots are lit and blocked. Matched on height, build and colouring so every setup translates exactly when the actor steps in. Rarely on camera.

02

Picture double

Appears on camera in place of the actor — over-the-shoulder angles, hands, driving shots and distance work. The match has to hold in the frame, not just under the lights.

03

Body double

Steps in for specific physical work — physique shots, movement, anything the schedule or the script keeps the actor from doing. Cast to match in close quarters.

04

Intimacy double

Performs intimate scenes in the actor's place, working to the intimacy coordinator's choreography — cast with consent, closed-set experience and a match that holds in frame.

05

Hand double

Close-up insert work — writing, driving, product handling, instruments. Matched on skin tone, size and detail, steady under a macro lens.

06

Stunt double

Takes the action the actor can't — falls, fights, vehicle work — under the stunt coordinator, matched closely enough to cut seamlessly with the performance.
