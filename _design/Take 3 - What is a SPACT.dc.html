01 · THE TERM
Where the word comes from

SPACT is short for Special Action. On UK productions, supporting artists are engaged on crowd agreements, and when a role calls for genuine physical ability the booking is designated special action — a SPACT for short. The term lives in call sheets, crowd budgets and AD department shorthand rather than in any glossary, which is why so few people outside a set have heard it.

In practice, you'll hear it used as a noun for the performer themselves: "we need six SPACTs for the fight in the market", "the riders are SPACTs, not doubles". It signals to everyone on set — ADs, stunt coordinators, HODs — that the person can genuinely do the thing the scene requires, and has been booked, briefed and paid accordingly.

02 · THE DISTINCTIONS
SPACT, Stunt Performer, Supporting Artist
The three roles sit on one spectrum of screen action, and the honest answer is that the lines are drawn by risk, pay and responsibility — not by talent.

03 · THE WORK
What SPACTs are booked to do

Anywhere a scene needs genuine ability in the background rather than a mime of it. Typical bookings we supply:

SCREEN FIGHTING
Background brawls, battle lines, gunmen in shootouts — choreographed contact that reads as real.
MOVEMENT
Ballroom, Latin, club and period social dance filling a floor with people who can actually dance it.
MILITARY & POLICE
Ex-forces performers who move, drill and handle weapons the way the real thing does.
RIDING & DRIVING
Horse riders, cyclists and precision drivers working around principal action.
SPECIALIST BODIES
Gymnasts, acrobats, circus and creature performers — anywhere physical control is the skill.
04 · THE BOOKING
How SPACTs are booked, and what to expect

SPACTs are booked through the crowd department, usually via an agency that specialises in them. The production sends a brief — the skill, the level, the look, the dates — and the agency returns a book of artists it can vouch for. Assessed skill levels matter more than showreels here: on the day, the performer either can or cannot do what the scene needs.

A production should expect a SPACT to arrive set-ready: briefed on the action, able to repeat it take after take, comfortable taking direction from ADs and coordinators, and clear about their own limits. Rates sit above standard background rates, agreed per the crowd agreement in place, with the special action noted on the chit.

Every SPACT on our books has been assessed and interviewed by the Take 3 team before joining. When we send someone to set, they are exactly who we say they are.
