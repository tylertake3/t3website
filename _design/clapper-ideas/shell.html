Credits page · interaction study

Five ways the
board could earn a click

Each board below is live — click it. These are flat CSS stand-ins for the real three.js object, so the point is the behaviour, not the render. Pick the ones worth building and I will put them into the real board.

01

Re-chalk between takes

The production already changes on a clap, but it changes instantly, which is why it does not register. Here the change happens while the sticks are shut: an eraser sweep wipes the marked values, they rewrite themselves one cell at a time, and the title cuts on a hard two-frame black. You never see it happen — you see the board come back up already re-marked, the way it does on set.

Click the board · watch the cells, not the sticks

02

Every production brings its own slate

A clap changes the object, not just the data. Four real slate types in rotation — the modern acrylic insert, a classic black board written in chalk, a wooden clapper with brass, and a digital smart-slate where everything is LED and nothing is handwritten. It makes the wall of credits feel like a range of productions rather than one template with the numbers swapped.

Click to change slate · acrylic → chalk → wood → digital

03

Throw the stick yourself

Drag the upper stick and it follows your pointer on the hinge; let go and it falls under its own weight, overshoots a hair and settles. A click still claps it. This is the one that makes people play — the board stops being a button and becomes an object you can pick up.

Drag the stick · or just click

04

Takes, then print

Clapping counts up: take 1, take 2, take 3 on the same production. On the third the board stamps PRINT and moves on. It gives the click somewhere to go instead of shuffling at random, and it teaches the interaction — you clap again because you want to see what take 2 does.

Click three times · watch TAKE

05

Peek at the back

Nothing currently tells you the board has a back. Hover its right edge and the corner lifts to show a sliver of the record underneath — a one-second tease that the turn exists. Cheap to build, and it answers "why would I scroll?".

Hover the right edge · no click needed
