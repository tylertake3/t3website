3. Where the term comes from

  • Small label above the heading: 01 · THE TERM
  • Heading: Where the word comes from

Paragraph1

  • The words before the link: SPACT is short for
  • The words inside that line shown in bold: Special Action
  • The words after the link: . On UK productions, supporting artists are engaged on crowd agreements, and when a role calls for genuine physical ability the booking is designated special action — a SPACT for short. The term lives in call sheets, crowd budgets and AD department shorthand rather than in any glossary, which is why so few people outside a set have heard it.
  • Second paragraph: In practice, you'll hear it used as a noun for the performer themselves: "we need six SPACTs for the fight in the market", "the riders are SPACTs, not doubles". It signals to everyone on set — ADs, stunt coordinators, HODs — that the person can genuinely do the thing the scene requires, and has been booked, briefed and paid accordingly.

4. How it differs

  • Small label above the heading: 02 · THE DISTINCTIONS
  • Heading: SPACT, Stunt Performer, Supporting Artist
  • Opening line: The three roles sit on one spectrum of screen action, and the honest answer is that the lines are drawn by risk, pay and responsibility — not by talent.
  • Select Prompt: SELECT A ROLE TO ISOLATE IT IN THE FRAME BELOW
  • Role Flag Label: ● IN FRAME — CLICK TO CLEAR

Roles (3 items)

Item 1

  • Heading: SUPPORTING ARTIST
  • Body copy: Populates the world of the scene. No special skill is required and none is asked for on camera. Booked through crowd casting on a standard background agreement.

Item 2

  • Heading: SPACT
  • Body copy: A supporting artist with a verified special skill, booked because of it. Performs real action — fights, dance, riding, drill — within limits agreed with the AD and stunt departments. Paid a special action rate above standard background.

Item 3

  • Heading: STUNT PERFORMER
  • Body copy: A registered professional engaged for hazardous action — falls, burns, wire work, crashes — under a stunt coordinator's supervision. Where the risk is real, the job is a stunt, and it is never a SPACT booking.

6. Booking

  • Small label above the heading: 04 · THE BOOKING
  • Heading: How SPACTs are booked, and what to expect

Paragraphs (3 items)

  • SPACTs are booked through the crowd department, usually via an agency that specialises in them. The production sends a brief — the skill, the level, the look, the dates — and the agency returns a book of artists it can vouch for. Assessed skill levels matter more than showreels here: on the day, the performer either can or cannot do what the scene needs.
  • A production should expect a SPACT to arrive set-ready: briefed on the action, able to repeat it take after take, comfortable taking direction from ADs and coordinators, and clear about their own limits. Rates sit above standard background rates, agreed per the crowd agreement in place, with the special action noted on the chit.
  • Every SPACT on our books has been assessed and interviewed by the Take 3 team before joining. When we send someone to set, they are exactly who we say they are.
